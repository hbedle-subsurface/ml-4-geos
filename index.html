Machine learning in the geosciences

An interactive lecture for incoming graduate students, School of Geosciences, University of Oklahoma

Concept

What we will cover

The vocabulary, the main types of machine learning, and then geophysics, sedimentology, geochemistry, minerals and paleontology, all on data where we can check the answer.

How each tab works

The Concept band at the top is what we talk through. Try it Out! below it is yours to click, and a short quiz closes each tab.

k-means

We choose k and place k centers. Every sample joins its nearest center, each center moves to the mean of its samples, and we repeat. The centers are just points in the data.

Self-organizing map

A grid of neurons stretches over the samples. Each sample pulls its closest neuron, and that neuron's grid neighbors, toward it. Neighboring neurons end up describing similar samples.

The data

Everything runs in the browser on 300 synthetic rock samples: sandstone, shale and limestone, each measured by gamma ray and density. The rock types are known, so we can check any method against them.

Try it Out!

Nobody told either method what a sandstone is. Group the same 300 samples with k-means and with a SOM, then turn on the true rock types and see how each one did.

What to do

  1. Pick k-means and press Watch it run. Follow the centers.
  2. Switch to the self-organizing map and press Watch it run. Look at the grid stretching over the samples.
  3. Turn on the true rock types and compare the two methods. Then change k and the number of neurons.
  4. Look at the panel on the right. What did each method build?
